How faithful is a fitted figure?

The workshop now fits its procedural adult figure to six body-scan previews. This note collects published methods for judging that fit and its renders, compares other ways to represent bodies, and reviews how to project images onto the editable mesh without baking in lighting. It ends with tests that fit the current scripts.

What the repository measures now

The survey below describes the working tree on 23 November 2026. The six completed studies record the later measurements and the source-image checks.

How the body is built

  • figure.js builds the figure as swept parts. Each knot is a ring with a height, half width, half depth and offset. Heights are derived from stature and head heights.
  • blended-skin.js voxelises the figure parts, blurs the occupancy and remeshes the result with marching cubes. Its grid has 88 samples in Anatomy and 72 otherwise. For a 1.72 m figure, with the code's padding, one vertical step at 88 samples is about 2.3 cm, or 1.3% of stature. That figure is estimated from the code, not measured. Contours finer than a few steps are smoothed before any fitting can reach them.
  • The section-profile.js adds horizontal scales and offsets at 14 height stations, applied to the mesh, clothes and joint pivots alike.
  • skin.js assigns up to four bone influences per vertex with linear blend skinning. expressions.js adds the only morph targets: blink, smile and brow lift. The body has no shape keys in the GLB.
  • Game export simplifies the mesh with meshoptimizer to a triangle budget and repacks every material into one atlas of 1024 or 2048 px. The atlas packs existing maps. It does not bake projections, and the export has no normal map.

How fidelity is scored

  • measured The profile study compared seat and chest depth with Blender Studio's CC0 base meshes as percentages of stature. Humanoid reference profiles.
  • The scripts/body-fidelity.py scores the figure against front, side, back and oblique previews of six adult scans listed in examples/body-references.json. It thresholds each preview, keeps the tallest component, samples 96 rows normalised to silhouette height, and compares occupied intervals per row. It fits 16 figure parameters on front and side with bounded Gauss–Newton steps. It then refines the 14 section stations: at each station it takes the median scale and offset that would move the outermost left and right edges of nearby front and side rows onto the reference. When the arms separate cleanly in both front masks, the script uses the central trunk interval at torso height. Front and side IoU, with an additional term for visible arm gaps, then choose between the proportion fit and a hand-blocked starting figure. The report gives default, coarse (without stations) and fitted scores per view, regional side scores, and the oblique view at three yaws with a 95th-percentile boundary distance. It also includes a flattened-trunk control and wrong-subject controls on the oblique and rear views.
  • scripts/body-sections.js cuts the editor mesh with horizontal planes and projects the section intervals orthographically.
  • scripts/capture-studies.py scores painted studies by silhouette distance at three views. visible-regions.py reports visible fractions per region. measure-garments.py measures garment–body intersection curves through clips. figure.test.js asserts arm length, wrist and shoulder heights as fractions of stature.

How artwork reaches the surface

  • turnaround.js reads a 2×2 sheet on magenta or transparency, keys it, erodes the mask by 2 px and grows foreground colour outwards. Each panel is projected orthographically without a camera model. The panel is placed by its lowest row, its height and the centre of its bounding box.
  • Each texel of a part's own UV grid chooses front or back by the sign of the normal's z component and a profile by its x component. The two are blended with fourth-power normal weights and sampled nearest-neighbour. An analytic elliptical depth buffer in rest pose removes pixels owned by other regions plus a 2 px border. Hidden texels are filled from the nearest visible pixel of the same region.
  • portrait-plate.js already does what the body projection lacks. It projects through a camera basis, tests depth and fades at grazing angles.
  • Lighting is not removed. The art prompt asks for even, diffuse lighting, and the painted material uses the texture partly as emissive colour. That reduces double shading but also makes the surface respond less to the game's light.

Gaps in the current approach

  1. The back score is not independent. The script makes the model's back view by mirroring its front projection. For an orthographic projection, that is exact. Any difference between front and back scores therefore comes from the reference images, through perspective, pose or lighting, not from the fit. The oblique view is the only held-out view of the fitted shape. That makes the oblique wrong-subject matrix the real identification test. The rear matrix repeats a fitted view.
  2. The camera models differ. The model is projected orthographically and the vendor supplies rendered previews without camera calibration. Normalising by bounding-box height absorbs scale but not the change of magnification with height and depth.
  3. Stance can absorb trunk error. Arm angle and foot spread are fitted together with widths. The section stations from 0.47 to 0.81 of stature also scale arm and joint positions, so a trunk width correction can move the arms. The editor now carries the arm axes between fitted endpoints to limit this effect.
  4. IoU hides small errors. Interval IoU per row changes little for a width error of a few percent. The completed run reports median and 95th-percentile boundary distances for every view. Inspect local errors at the wrists and ankles alongside those statistics.
  5. The front and side scores are in-sample. The section stations are set from the same front and side rows that are then scored, and the same scores choose the candidate. A rise from coarse to fitted on those views is expected by construction. Judge the station refinement by the coarse-to-fitted change on the oblique view, and by girths or 3D distances where a mesh reference exists.
  6. Outer edges can belong to the arms. Station scales come from the outermost left and right edges of each row. Where the arms hang beside the trunk, between roughly 0.47 and 0.81 of stature in an A-pose, the front-view edge is an arm. A station scale then widens or narrows the trunk ring to place the arm. Use the inner trunk edges where a gap is visible, or exclude rows where arm and trunk touch.
  7. Two vendor entries have inconsistent filenames. The f01 front URL is in a folder named Shaded Female 07, though its file name says F01. The f08 images are pose 2, though its page is pose 1. The product pages were checked against the displayed images. The reference manifest records both naming discrepancies and SHA-256 hashes for the exact plates used.
  8. Licensing. The previews are a vendor's product images. Keep them in the local cache as the script does. Do not commit them or publish derived images without checking the vendor's terms. The project's agent instructions require recording the origin and licence of contributed art.
  9. Keep the scripts with the study and the fits local. examples/reference-studies.js and the default scoring mode read examples/reference-fits.json where it is installed. The scoring scripts and result JSON accompany the study. The fitted parameters derive from the vendor's previews and are local reference material, so a clean checkout fits again with --fit.

Comparing with a reference

A reference comparison needs three decisions: the ground truth, the alignment, and a distance that reflects the error that matters. Report each separately. A good number from a poor alignment does not show fidelity.

Geometry, when a scan mesh is available

published Metro compares two triangle meshes by sampling one surface and finding the closest point on the other. It reports the mean and maximum error in both directions, where the maximum two-sided value is the Hausdorff distance [Metro]. Single-image body reconstruction papers report the same family. PIFu used point-to-surface distance, Chamfer distance and normal consistency [PIFu]. FAUST showed that registering real scans is still error-prone. Its painted subjects gave correspondences that could be checked [FAUST].

  • Scan to model distance shows where the fitted figure misses the body. Model to scan distance shows added volume. One-sided Chamfer averages hide which of these happened.
  • Report the median, the 90th percentile and the maximum. A single maximum is dominated by fingers, hair and the scan's floor plate.
  • Express distances in millimetres after scaling both to the same stature, and as a percentage of stature. Break them down by region: head and neck, trunk, arms, hands, legs, feet.
  • published Plain Chamfer distance is insensitive to uneven point density. Density-aware Chamfer was proposed for that reason [DCD]. Sample both surfaces uniformly by area to avoid the issue.
  • Normal consistency, the mean absolute cosine between matched normals, catches lumpy or faceted surfaces whose distance error is small.

Measurements a tailor would take

published ISO 7250-1 defines body dimensions such as stature, chest, waist and hip girths and segment lengths. ISO 20685-1 specifies how to check that dimensions extracted from 3D scans agree with those manual definitions [ISO 7250-1] [ISO 20685-1]. Allen, Curless and Popović fitted a template to 250 CAESAR scans with sparse markers, then used the consistent parameterisation for texture transfer and statistics [Allen 2003].

proposed Extract chest, waist, hip and thigh girths, shoulder breadth, and crotch, knee and shoulder heights from both meshes on planes at the same fraction of stature. Girths are perimeters of the horizontal section loops. The existing section script already produces those loops, but it currently projects them to intervals. Girths reveal depth errors that a front silhouette cannot see.

published The ANSUR II tables give 93 measurements for 4,082 men and 1,986 women. The survey authors advise analysing the two groups separately. The 3D scans were not released [ANSUR II]. Those tables make a plausibility check, not ground truth for a particular scan.

Renders and silhouettes, when only images exist

The current six references are shaded product renders, not meshes. In that case:

  • Silhouette IoU is robust and simple, but it saturates. A 2% error in width moves IoU by little more than 2%. It also cannot see a concavity that does not reach the outline.
  • Contour distance compares the outline itself. Take the symmetric mean and 95th-percentile distance between the two boundaries as a fraction of stature. It responds to small width errors that IoU hides.
  • Per-row edges describe where an outline is wrong: the left and right extents, occupied width and gaps at each height. The fitting descriptor in scripts/body-fidelity.py already uses this. Report it as a plot against height, not only as a summed residual.
  • published Shaded comparisons such as SSIM [SSIM], LPIPS [LPIPS] and FLIP [FLIP] assume the two images are aligned and rendered with comparable light. FLIP was designed for rendered images against references and gives a per-pixel error map. LPIPS was trained on human similarity judgments and tracks them better than PSNR or SSIM in its authors' tests.
  • proposed To compare shading without matching the product lighting, render normal maps of the fitted figure. Compare them with normals estimated from a mesh reference where one exists. With only a shaded preview, use LPIPS or FLIP on clay renders as a secondary score after silhouette alignment, and treat it as a ranking signal.

Checking without a reference

Most edits in the workshop have no ground truth. Checks without a reference catch broken output, but they cannot show a likeness.

Geometry and rig checks

  • Mesh validity: no non-manifold edges inside a closed part, no degenerate triangles, consistent winding, and no self-intersection of body parts at rest. Clothes must stay outside the body by a minimum clearance. scripts/measure-garments.py already does this at rest and in motion for garments.
  • Anthropometric plausibility: give each ratio in the figure request (shoulder to hip breadth, leg length to stature, head heights) a z-score against ANSUR II. Flag values beyond about three standard deviations. They may be intended, but they should be deliberate.
  • Volume under motion: published linear blend skinning collapses volume at twisting and strongly bent joints. Dual quaternion skinning was proposed to avoid that collapse at little cost [Kavan 2007]. proposed Sweep the elbow, shoulder, hip and knee through their clip ranges. Record the change in section area near each joint and the minimum distance between the forearm and upper arm surfaces.
  • Symmetry: mirror the rest mesh across the sagittal plane and measure the distance to itself. Asymmetry can be intentional, but an asymmetry introduced by the fitter is a bug.

Image checks

  • published NIQE and BRISQUE score images without a reference using natural-scene statistics [NIQE] [BRISQUE]. They were built for photographs with distortions such as blur and compression. Clay renders on a flat background are not natural scenes, so their scores here are unvalidated.
  • published FID and KID compare distributions of image features [FID] [KID]. Both need hundreds to thousands of images per set to be stable. Six subjects are far too few. Use them only for generator-wide studies with many seeds.
  • published Among four subjective methods, forced-choice pairwise comparison gave the most accurate results for the effort [Mantiuk 2012]. proposed For likeness, show a viewer the scan preview and two fitted renders side by side in random order, and ask which matches the body better. Ten viewers and 30 pairs are enough for a pilot. Include pairs where one candidate is a bad control.

Pose, scale and camera

A figure can match its outline in the wrong pose, at the wrong scale or through the wrong camera. Each needs its own normalisation and its own check.

  • Scale. Normalise to stature measured from the floor to the vertex of the head, without hair. The script divides by the height of the silhouette's bounding box. That works for bald, barefoot figures. A raised heel, a hairstyle or a floor shadow in the preview changes every row. Report stature-normalised and absolute results separately when the source states the subject's height.
  • Arm and foot stance. The A-pose arm angle changes the apparent shoulder width and the gap between arm and trunk. The fitter includes stance.arms and stance.feet as free parameters, so a stance error can be absorbed by trunk widths. proposed Report trunk widths only on rows where the arm does not touch the trunk, and report the fitted arm angle beside each subject.
  • Camera. The workshop projects orthographically. The previews are perspective renders with unknown focal length and camera height. With perspective, parts nearer the camera appear larger. The feet and the head also appear with slightly different scales when the camera is at chest height. published For real photographs, a planar target gives intrinsics and lens distortion [Zhang 2000]. For product images, estimate the camera from the figure: proposed sweep a perspective camera at 2–8 m distance, 0.9–1.4 m height, and accept the camera that maximises IoU of the default figure. Then fit the shape with that camera fixed. If the fitted shape changes by more than the camera sweep's spread, camera error dominates the result.
  • Oblique views. The script already scores the oblique view at its nominal yaw and at ⅔ and 4⁄3 of that yaw. That spread is a useful error bar. Report it beside the score.
  • Pose transfer. A static fit says nothing about motion. proposed Run the fitted figures through Idle, Walk, Crouch and Reach and record garment penetrations, joint volume change and foot sliding. Compare them with the default archetype's values.

Projecting images onto geometry

Projection paints a surface point with the colour found where that point lands in a source image. Each step below is a place where errors enter.

1 · Cameras and calibration

  • A turnaround drawn as orthographic views can be projected orthographically, but only if the artist kept the views orthographic and the same scale. Generated four-view sheets often break both assumptions.
  • published Real photographs need intrinsics and lens distortion. A printed planar target photographed from two or more orientations is enough [Zhang 2000].
  • Without a calibrated camera, register the image to the mesh with corresponding landmarks such as pupils, chin, navel, nipples, knees and ankles. Solve for a camera from them and check the reprojection error in pixels. The workshop's paired facial landmarks follow this pattern.

2 · Visibility and occlusion

  • Render a depth buffer from each source camera. A surface point receives colour only if its depth matches that buffer within a tolerance. Without that test, a forearm in front of the hip prints its colour onto the hip and onto the back.
  • Silhouette edges are unreliable. Shrink each view's usable mask by a few pixels so background and outline pixels do not bleed in. This matters with a magenta or white background.
  • Points that no view sees need inpainting in UV space or a fallback material. Mark them in a coverage map so they can be reviewed, rather than leaving them silently filled.

3 · Choosing and blending views

  • Weight each view by how directly it sees the surface. A cosine between surface normal and view direction raised to a power is common. Also lower the weight near silhouette edges and where the image resolution per surface area is low.
  • published Averaging misregistered views produces ghosting. Lempitsky and Ivanov instead chose one view per face with a Markov random field energy and then levelled the seams in the gradient domain [Lempitsky 2007]. Waechter et al. extended that to large scenes with photometric outliers and global colour adjustment [Waechter 2014]. Gradient-domain blending follows Poisson image editing [Pérez 2003].
  • published When views are generated rather than photographed, they disagree in content as well as colour. TEXTure divides each new view into regions to keep, refine and generate, and paints the model view by view [TEXTure].

4 · Baking to UVs

  • Do the projection in the pose that the image shows, then write the result into the rest-pose UV atlas. Projecting a posed image onto the rest mesh makes the texture slide when the figure moves.
  • Pad every UV island by at least as many texels as the coarsest mip level needs. Otherwise colours from neighbouring islands bleed in at a distance.
  • published Blender's selected-to-active bake casts rays inward from a cage. A custom cage must share the base mesh's topology [Blender baking]. The same idea applies when a fitted garment sits over the body: bake each layer from its own surface.

5 · Avoiding baked lighting

  • A projected image contains light and shade. If the game also lights the surface, shadows appear twice and highlights stay fixed as the figure turns.
  • Prefer flat, even, shadowless source art. Ask the image generator for albedo-like colour, and check the four views against each other before projection.
  • published Unity's de-lighting tool estimated and removed lighting from photogrammetry textures using ambient occlusion, normals and bent normals [Unity de-lighting]. Learned intrinsic decomposition predicts albedo from one image. One recent model samples several plausible albedo and material maps, but it was trained on indoor scenes [Kocsis 2024].
  • proposed A cheap test: compute the ratio between the projected colour and a clay render of the mesh under the source's estimated light. If dividing by that clay shading flattens the albedo, the source carried that lighting.

6 · Wardrobe

  • Project each garment layer separately with its own depth. Garment pixels must not reach the skin underneath. Use the garment's depth as an occluder when painting the body.
  • Skin hidden by clothing in every view has no source. When the outfit changes, those regions appear. Either paint them from a body-only source or fill them with a matched skin material and label them in the coverage map.
  • Recut and refitted garments change their UV layout. Store projections as source images plus camera registration so they can be rebaked, instead of storing only the baked atlas.
